Creative brief — "Two Lines to Your Past Self" (make.fun, $2, u-suga)

GOAL
Write two lines that win a head-to-head judge comparison: the sharpest
warning to your past self about one specific moment, with the clearest
cause and effect.

THE TEXT (exactly two lines, one sentence each)
Line 1 — "Don't sign the car loan at the bank counter on Tuesday morning,
even though the manager smiles and says the payments are small."
Line 2 — "That signature bought five years of debt for a car you parked
for good eleven months later."

WHY THIS MOMENT
- One specific moment, not a life lesson: a decision (signing a car
  loan), a place (the bank counter), a time (Tuesday morning), and a
  sentence said out loud ("the payments are small").
- Cause and effect, not a restatement: line 2 traces what the signature
  caused — five years of debt for a car abandoned eleven months later —
  with numbers that make the cost concrete.
- Voice: plain, direct, addressed to one person ("you"), no cleverness
  for its own sake. The twist is the gap between the small monthly
  payment the manager promised and the five-year debt it became.

CONSTRAINTS (from the competition rules)
- Output: $MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR/entry.txt, UTF-8, 1–280 characters.
- Exactly two lines; each line exactly one sentence.
- No text addressed to the judge, no meta-commentary, no gimmick —
  the entry wins on concreteness and cause-and-effect alone.

PROGRAM
main.py (Python 3.13, stdlib only, no network, no randomness) holds the
text as a constant, validates it against the 280-character gate and the
two-line shape, and writes it deterministically — two runs produce
byte-identical output.
